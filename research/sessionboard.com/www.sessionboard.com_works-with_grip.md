## What Sessionboard delivers natively

Keep Grip mid-contract when you need to — Sessionboard owns call for papers, speaker operations, and content marketing on the program graph.

## Grip — their lane

- **Registration and event operations**
Networking, matchmaking, and event platform delivery for enterprise programs on Grip.

- **Attendee-facing event experience**
Buyer-standard UX and ops teams already trained on Grip.

- **Enterprise or portfolio contracts**
Multi-event programs standardized on Grip before program depth is evaluated.


## Sessionboard — built in

- **Abstract and reviewer operations**
Multi-round review, COI, and chair self-service on the program graph.

- **Speaker CRM through show day**
Materials, bios, and agenda truth — not re-keyed from registration exports.

- **Content Marketing stack**
Transcription, Attendee Hub, Create, and Amplify on session records.


## How the data flows

Program and session data live in Sessionboard — with optional sync to and from Grip.

- Attendee registers on Grip — registration record stays the system of record.
- Accepted speakers and sessions live in Sessionboard — program team works without CSV re-keying.
- Native continuous sync (integration\_type: grip) keeps event and session data aligned — confirm field map with sales engineering.
- Live transcription and Attendee Hub deliver content from Sessionboard session records.

**Native connector:** Grip ↔ Sessionboard native continuous sync shipped (integration\_type: grip in web-api). Program layer can start via import while registration remains on Grip.

## Native first — add their tool only if you must

Sessionboard already includes transcription, attendee experiences, and content marketing. A separate subscription makes sense only in narrow cases.

#### Keep Grip only when

- Multi-year Grip contract is non-negotiable
- Ops team standardized on Grip for registration or event delivery

#### Sessionboard alone — usually the answer

- Abstract committee pain is the bottleneck — not registration UX
- Need transcription, clips, and advocacy tied to program sessions
- Speaker identity must persist across years on one CRM
