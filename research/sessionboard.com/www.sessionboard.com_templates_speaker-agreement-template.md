## Quick answer

A speaker agreement should cover five areas: deliverables with real deadlines, recording and content-use consent by channel, IP terms where the speaker keeps ownership and the event gets a license, cancellation and substitution on both sides, and compensation or expense terms even when the answer is none. Collect consent at acceptance, not backstage. Practical checklist, not legal advice.

## Deliverables & deadlines

The section that prevents the week-before chase cycle. Name dates, not 'in advance.'

- Session title, format, duration, and date/time as accepted — with agreement that timing may shift within the event dates
- Materials deadline: slides or poster in stated format by a named date (typically 1–2 weeks out), through the stated channel
- Bio, headshot, and disclosure forms due at confirmation — not at print deadline
- Rehearsal or tech-check attendance where the format requires it, with the scheduled window
- Arrival time on event day: when and where to check in before the session

## Recording, transcription & content use

The clauses that unlock post-event content — or quietly forbid it if never asked.

- Consent to record the session: audio, video, and photography, named explicitly
- Consent to transcribe and caption — transcription is a separate use from the recording itself
- Named distribution channels: attendee replay portal, public clips, quote cards, social promotion — consent per channel, not one vague 'marketing purposes'
- Duration of use: this event cycle only, or ongoing library use
- Embargo or restriction option: a field where the speaker can restrict specific content (unpublished data, patient cases) rather than refusing wholesale
- Name, likeness, and bio use in event marketing — before the event, not just after

## Content ownership & IP

- Speaker retains ownership of their materials — events license use, they don't take copyright
- License granted to the event: reproduce, distribute, and display materials in the named channels for the named duration
- Speaker warrants the content is theirs to present — third-party images, data, and case material are cleared
- Original-content expectation: the session materially matches the accepted abstract; substitutions of topic require approval
- No-selling clause where your event needs it: educational sessions are not vendor pitches, stated plainly

## Cancellation, substitution & compensation

- Speaker cancellation: notice required, and whether a qualified substitute may be proposed (event approves)
- Event cancellation or session cut: what the speaker is owed — expenses incurred, fee portion, or nothing, but written
- Force majeure and format change: the session may move to virtual or recorded delivery under named circumstances
- Compensation stated even when zero: honorarium amount and payment timing, or explicit no-fee language
- Expenses: what is covered (travel, lodging, registration), caps, and the reimbursement process with receipt deadlines
- Registration status: whether speaking includes event registration, and for which days

## Signature & collection process

- Sign at acceptance, as part of the confirmation flow — the moment of maximum goodwill and attention
- One document per speaker per event, versioned; a co-presented session needs each presenter's signature
- Store the executed agreement and its consent flags on the speaker record — the AV team and content team need to query 'can we record this session' without reading PDFs
- Renewal events: returning faculty re-confirm annually; last year's consent does not automatically carry

## Frequently asked questions

What should a speaker agreement include?▼

Five areas: deliverables with real dates, recording and content-use consent by channel, IP terms where the speaker keeps ownership and the event gets a license, cancellation and substitution terms on both sides, and compensation or expense language even when the answer is none.

Do unpaid speakers need an agreement?▼

Yes — most of the value is non-financial: recording consent, materials deadlines, content rights, and cancellation expectations apply identically to unpaid faculty. The compensation clause simply says no fee is payable.

When should speakers sign the agreement?▼

At acceptance, inside the confirmation flow, alongside bio, headshot, and disclosure collection. Asking backstage for recording consent is how events lose their best session content.

Who owns the speaker's presentation content?▼

The speaker, almost always. The agreement grants the event a license to record, reproduce, and distribute through named channels for a named duration — ownership transfer is neither needed nor reasonable to ask.

What happens if a speaker cancels close to the event?▼

Whatever the agreement says — which is the point. Standard terms require notice as early as possible, allow the event to approve a qualified substitute, and end any fee obligation. The agenda-side fix is a session record that updates every surface when the swap happens.

Is a speaker agreement the same as a speaker release form?▼

A release covers recording and likeness consent only; an agreement covers that plus deliverables, IP, cancellation, and payment. Small events sometimes use a release alone — once you record, transcribe, and republish session content, you want the full agreement.
