## From accepted abstract to published gallery

The gallery reads from the same program records as your call for papers and speaker portal. Publishing is a toggle, and the rest follows.

- Abstracts accepted through your call for papers become sessions with authors attached. The gallery reads those records directly, so titles, authors, and program numbers are always current.
- Authors upload their poster in the speaker portal they already use for bios and headshots. If your meeting requires publication consent, the upload captures it, with versioned consent text.
- You pick what the gallery includes by track, format, or session, choose the fields each card shows, set your colors and logo, and flip it live. The public link and a print-ready QR code are one click each.
- Attendees search instantly across titles, authors, and program numbers, filter by track or format, bookmark a personal list, and view posters crisply in the browser on any device. Every filtered view has its own shareable URL.
- Each author gets a share kit in their portal: a direct link to their poster, a QR code, and a live view of their own page. Your authors become the distribution channel.
- Afterward, the analytics tab shows poster opens, time spent, the most-viewed posters, attendee questions, and the searches that returned nothing. That last one tells you what your audience wanted and your program lacked.

## Frequently asked questions

How the Poster Gallery works, what it costs authors in effort (nothing), and where it fits.

How do posters get into the gallery?▼

Authors upload in the Sessionboard speaker portal, the same place they submit bios and headshots. Accepted abstracts already carry the title, authors, and program number, so the poster file is the only thing anyone adds.

Do attendees need an account or an app?▼

No. The gallery is a web link. Attendees open it from your event site, an email, or a QR code on signage, and search immediately. Bookmarks work without a login.

How does consent work for medical meetings?▼

You write the consent language once and turn on the requirement. Authors accept it during upload, the acceptance is stored with a version stamp, and posters without consent stay out of the public gallery until it is recorded.

Can we keep posters hidden until a specific date?▼

Yes. Set an embargo date and choose whether the gallery hides entirely or shows title-only teaser cards until it lifts. Withdrawn abstracts never publish at all.

What do the AI summaries do?▼

With one click, the gallery writes a two-to-three sentence plain-language summary for each poster from its title and abstract, so a general attendee can decide whether to open a specialist poster. You trigger it, review it, and nothing generates without you.

What analytics do we get?▼

Poster opens, average time spent, most-viewed posters, questions submitted to authors, and the search terms that returned no results. The zero-result list is the clearest signal of content your audience wanted and your program did not have.

Can sponsors be part of the gallery?▼

Yes. Each gallery has a sponsor logo and link slot in the header, which gives you a sellable placement on one of the most-visited pages of the meeting.

We already use a dedicated ePoster vendor. Why switch?▼

Dedicated tools re-import your program data and manage authors as a separate population. Because the gallery reads your live program, it is current by construction, and your authors keep one portal. See our comparison pages for an honest fit assessment.
