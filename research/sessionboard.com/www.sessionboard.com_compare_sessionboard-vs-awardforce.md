## Quick answer

Sessionboard is program-native abstract and speaker software for medical and scientific societies — multi-round review, speaker portal, live transcription, Attendee Hub, and Advocacy on the session record. AwardForce fits when awards and grants are the only program — not when awards and annual meeting sessions should share one speaker CRM.

## Sound like your team?

Match your role to the job Sessionboard solves — program ops, content reuse, or marketing attribution.

AwardForce closes the season — winner CSV re-keyed into conference speaker lists; faculty CRM should persist automatically.

### Conference program director

Award recipients should present at the meeting — acceptance should open speaker portal on the same record.

### Marketing director

Winner gallery without registration attribution — Advocacy should fire from acceptance on the program graph.

### Portfolio governance lead

Board asks for year-over-year awards and advocate reporting — you need event-over-event rollups, not per-season exports.

## AwardForce — where it ends

- **Awards and grants depth**
Multi-category judging, eligibility rules, and winner communications.

- **Standalone awards contracts**
Procurement buying awards without a conference module mandate.

Applicant and judge portals tuned for awards-only programs.


### The narrow case

- Awards-only program with no annual meeting or speaker content requirements
- Grants administration is the sole scope for three-plus years

The way forward

## Sessionboard — program, CRM & content

- **Speaker CRM across awards and conference**
Nominees, judges, winners, and session faculty share one record — persists event over event, not exported each season.

- **Conference plus awards wedge**
Enter via awards when conference contract is locked — expand to call for papers on the same faculty graph.

- **Live content and advocacy**
Transcription, Attendee Hub, clips, and Advocacy on ceremony and winner sessions — tied to CRM history.


### When Sessionboard wins

- Society runs awards and annual meeting but data lives in two systems
- Winners should promote sessions with tracked registration attribution
- Need one speaker CRM across awards nominees, judges, and conference faculty
- Portfolio reporting requires SME and advocate history across multiple events

## What Sessionboard owns

Program, speaker CRM, content — plus Advocacy and Dispatch marketing tools competitors treat as afterthoughts.

### Program layer

Call for papers (CFP), multi-round review, chair workflows, conflict of interest (COI) tracking, and published agenda — work competitors leave in spreadsheets.

### Speaker CRM

One faculty record: portal, bio, materials, and communications across years — not a fresh CSV every submission cycle.

### Content marketing

Live transcription, Media Library, clips, Attendee Hub, and Create on the session record you already manage.

### Advocacy

Peer shares from speakers, sponsors, and faculty — tracked links to registration when sessions are accepted or confirmed.

### Dispatch

Internal team distribution — automated share recipes for staff when content is ready, beside Hootsuite or Sprout for always-on social.

## Frequently asked questions

Evaluating Sessionboard against AwardForce for the program layer — not a forced rip-and-replace.

Does Sessionboard replace AwardForce?▼

When you want robust awards with member SSO, dedupe, merge, and optional conference on one CRM — yes. AwardForce may stay if you need only a narrow judging season with no identity graph.

Can we start with Awards only?▼

Yes — many customers run Awards without a conference. SSO, dedupe, and multi-round judging work standalone; add call for papers when ready.

Does Sessionboard support multi-round judging?▼

Yes — awards judging workflows with rubrics and chair tools on the same platform as abstract review.

What about conference abstract review?▼

Same platform — call for papers, review, speaker portal, and content capture share speaker CRM with Awards.

Do judges carry forward year over year?▼

Yes — judge and reviewer records persist on faculty CRM; chairs clone assignments instead of rebuilding volunteer lists from email.
