## Quick answer

A call for abstracts runs in five stages: announce topics and dates, collect structured submissions, review in scored rounds, notify and confirm presenters, and build the program. Most calls fail in the gaps — vague topics, forms that collect the wrong fields, reviewer chase emails, and acceptance handoffs that fall back to spreadsheets.

## Running the call?

The same five stages look different depending on the seat you're in — match the guide to the problem in front of you.

### Program chair / committee

Submissions arrive off-topic and uneven — the announcement and form structure decide review quality before a single reviewer logs in.

### Education director (med/sci)

Blind review, conflict-of-interest checks, and disclosure collection are requirements, not preferences — the process has to prove fairness.

### Event / speaker operations

Acceptance is where the real work starts — confirmations, bios, AV specs, and materials should flow from the same record, not a fresh spreadsheet.

### Marketing

The call is a campaign — announcement copy, deadline pushes, and accepted-speaker promotion all need dates on the same timeline.

Match the tooling to submission volume, review complexity, and what happens after acceptance — the stage most setups ignore.

## Evaluation checklist

- Does the announcement name specific topic tracks with examples, or will vague themes attract off-target submissions?
- Does the form collect what review needs (track, format, learning objectives, disclosures) — not just title and abstract text?
- Is the timeline realistic — 8–12 weeks open, review windows with buffer, decisions before speakers book travel?
- Can reviewers be assigned by topic with conflicts excluded automatically, or does the chair match names in a spreadsheet?
- Is scoring structured (rubric, blind where required) so decisions survive an author's appeal?
- What happens at acceptance — do confirmations, bios, AV specs, and materials flow from the same record?
- Do declined authors get a useful response — feedback or a poster/alternate-format offer — that keeps them submitting next year?
- Does the whole thing persist — submissions, scores, and speaker history — so the next edition starts warm?

## Frequently asked questions

What is a call for abstracts?▼

The open invitation for prospective presenters to submit session, paper, or poster proposals for review before a conference program is built. Also called a call for papers, call for speakers, or call for proposals depending on the field — the pipeline is the same: announce, collect, review, decide, and build the program.

How long should a call for abstracts stay open?▼

Eight to twelve weeks is the common window for association and scientific meetings, with the deadline at least four to five months before the event so review, notifications, and speaker confirmations happen before travel booking. Expect a submission spike in the final week regardless of the window.

What should an abstract submission form collect?▼

Everything review and program build need, collected once: title, structured abstract, topic track, preferred format, author and co-presenter details, learning objectives where CE applies, and disclosures. Every field missing at submission becomes a chase email later.

How does abstract review usually work?▼

Reviewers are assigned by topic with conflicts of interest excluded, score against a rubric — relevance, rigor, clarity, novelty — often blind to author identity, and the committee decides on ranked results. Larger programs run two rounds: a broad screen, then a program-fit pass.

What is the difference between a call for abstracts and a call for speakers?▼

A call for abstracts asks for the content first — a written proposal reviewed on merit, standard for scientific and educational programs. A call for speakers recruits the person, common for keynotes and industry panels. Many conferences run both against the same program timeline.

What happens after abstracts are accepted?▼

The stage most calls fumble: acceptance notifications with confirm-by deadlines, then bios, headshots, AV requirements, disclosures, and presentation uploads. On Sessionboard this flows from the same record into a speaker portal; in spreadsheet setups it becomes a second, error-prone data collection.

What software runs a call for abstracts?▼

Under ~50 submissions, forms and a spreadsheet work. Scored or blind review at volume needs a review tool — Ex Ordo and Oxford Abstracts stop at the decision, suite modules like Cvent and Cadmium depend on vendor configuration, and Sessionboard runs the call plus everything after acceptance on one record.
