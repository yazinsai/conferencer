## Quick answer

Conference planning runs on dependencies, not dates: the call for abstracts gates the program, the program gates marketing, and speaker confirmations gate everything printed. This checklist organizes the 12 months by phase — foundation (12–9 months out), program build (9–5), production (5–2), and show-ready (final 8 weeks) — with the program-side tasks generic checklists skip.

## Foundation — 12 to 9 months out

- Set the goal that decides trade-offs later: attendance, revenue, member value, or CE delivery — pick the primary
- Lock dates against competing events, holidays, and your industry's travel calendar
- Contract the venue with room block, AV, and F&B tied to realistic attendance — last year's actual, not the stretch goal
- Set the budget with a 10–15% contingency line that nobody is allowed to pre-spend
- Recruit the program committee and agree the topic tracks — vague tracks now become off-target submissions later
- Choose the program stack: submissions, review, speaker management, agenda — the systems decision that haunts or helps every later phase
- Open sponsorship sales with inventory defined: booths, sessions, amplification packages, receptions

## Program build — 9 to 5 months out

The phase generic checklists skip. This is where the conference is actually made.

- Launch the call for abstracts 8–12 weeks open, with structured forms that collect what review needs (track, format, objectives, disclosures)
- Promote the call in waves — launch, mid-point, final week — through your speaker CRM and member channels
- Assign reviewers by topic with conflicts excluded; score against a rubric, blind where your field expects it
- Run decisions on a hard calendar: authors need acceptance 4–5 months out to book travel
- Send acceptances with confirm-by deadlines; collect bios, headshots, AV needs, and disclosures in the same flow — not four chase emails
- Build the agenda from accepted sessions, checking the grid per speaker, per room, and per track
- Confirm keynotes and invited speakers with contracts, fees, and recording consent in writing
- Open registration the day the program is strong enough to sell — session-level agenda published

## Production — 5 to 2 months out

- Marketing runs on the program: session and speaker promotion, not generic save-the-dates — give speakers tracked share assets
- Collect presentations and posters with format checks and a real deadline one week before show
- Lock AV orders per room from actual session formats — the panel needs four mics, the workshop needs power at tables
- Publish the mobile app / online agenda from the same data as the printed program — divergence here is the classic day-one embarrassment
- Brief moderators and session chairs: timing, Q&A handling, and the overrun protocol
- Staff plan with names per role per slot: registration desk, room monitors, speaker ready room, floaters
- CE/CME paperwork filed: learning objectives, disclosures, evaluation mechanics approved by the accreditor

## Show-ready — final 8 weeks

- Freeze the program two weeks out — after that, changes go through one owner and update every surface from one record
- Build run of show docs per stage and rehearse transitions the day before
- Print with a change plan: what happens to signage, program book, and app when a speaker drops day-of
- Confirm every speaker one week out with their room, time, AV setup, and where to check in
- Pre-write the post-event: survey ready to send within 24 hours, content capture (recording, transcription) confirmed per room
- Debrief scheduled for the week after — while the team still remembers, with the survey data in the room

## Frequently asked questions

How far in advance should you plan a conference?▼

Twelve months for a program-driven conference — the call for abstracts alone needs 8–12 weeks open plus review time, and authors need acceptance 4–5 months out to book travel. Smaller single-track events compress to 6 months; the dependency order stays the same.

What is the correct order of conference planning tasks?▼

Dates and venue first, then the call for abstracts, then review and acceptances, then the agenda, then registration and marketing — because marketing sells the program, and the program doesn't exist until review finishes. Logistics runs parallel but never gates content.

When should conference registration open?▼

When there is a program worth buying — ideally a session-level agenda. Early-bird windows work best against a visible program; registration opened against a bare save-the-date converts your most loyal attendees only.

What do most conference checklists miss?▼

The program layer: structured abstract collection, reviewer assignment with conflict checks, acceptance-to-confirmation flows, and speaker material collection. Generic checklists cover venue and catering, then compress the actual conference content into one line item.

How do you handle speaker changes close to the event?▼

Assume they will happen and design for it: freeze the program two weeks out, route changes through one owner, and keep agenda, app, program book, and signage generated from one session record so a swap updates every surface at once.

What software does conference planning need?▼

Registration, plus a program layer for submissions, review, speaker management, and agenda build. Suites bundle a thin version of the program side; teams with real review workflows typically run a dedicated platform like Sessionboard beside their registration system.
