## Vendor landscape

No single winner — match the tool to your trigger, data ownership, and team workflow.

| Vendor | Best when | Tradeoffs | Pricing note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sessionboard | Abstracts and awards share one contact graph with the meeting itself. Call for papers, blind multi-round review, judging, speaker CRM, portal, and session transcription run on one record the committee configures itself. | Registration and CE credit stay on your existing stack; Sessionboard runs beside them rather than replacing them. | Licensing follows meeting and submission volume; a scoped quote takes one demo. |
| Cadmium (eventScribe) | The society already runs education and CE products on the Cadmium suite and wants abstracts on the same vendor contract. | Committee changes route through vendor configuration, and the speaker layer beyond review often ends up in email. | Suite pricing across modules. |
| Ex Ordo | Academic conferences need submission and review only, with no awards program and no speaker operations worth software. | Everything after acceptance needs another system, and each edition starts from a fresh import. | Priced per conference on submission volume. |
| Award Force | A standalone awards program with no connected meeting needs entry, judging, and winner management. | No abstract or speaker side at all, so societies running both end up with two systems and two contact databases. | Tiered on entry volume and features. |

## Evaluation checklist

- Do abstracts, awards, and faculty share one contact record, or does each program keep its own database?
- Can the committee change forms, rubrics, and reports itself mid-season, without a vendor ticket?
- What happens after acceptance? Speaker portal, AV specs, and automated reminders, or a staff inbox?
- Do reviewer and judge histories persist across years, so assignments start from experience instead of a blank list?
- Is session content captured? Transcription and a media library turn the program into a year-round asset.
- If entrants and submitters are members, does the platform support SSO against your AMS?

## Frequently asked questions

What is the most common reason teams leave OpenWater?▼

Scope. OpenWater handles forms and review well, and stops there. Teams running a meeting behind the forms end up managing speaker readiness, faculty history, and session content in email and spreadsheets, then go looking for a platform that runs the whole arc.

Is Sessionboard a full OpenWater replacement?▼

For abstracts and awards, yes: call for papers, custom forms, blind multi-round review, judging workflows, and winner management run natively, plus the speaker CRM, portal, and transcription OpenWater does not offer. Grants programs are the one OpenWater use Sessionboard does not chase.

Can we keep OpenWater for awards and use Sessionboard for the meeting?▼

You can run the split, and some teams do for a cycle. The cost is two databases: the member who submits an abstract, judges an award, and speaks at the meeting exists three times. Consolidating on one contact graph is usually why the evaluation started.

How does pricing compare across these platforms?▼

None of the four publish full list pricing; quotes follow scope and volume. Sessionboard pricing follows meeting and submission volume, and a scoped quote takes one demo. Put it beside your OpenWater renewal before you sign it.

What happens to entry and reviewer history from past OpenWater cycles?▼

It becomes the starting point. Past submissions, entrants, and reviewer assignments import into the speaker and contact CRM, so returning participants see prefilled profiles and staff see who delivered.
