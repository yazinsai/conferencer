## Why teams choose Sessionboard

Where Linklings stops, and where program teams keep going with Sessionboard.

## Linklings — where it ends

- **Peer review tenure**
Twenty years of single- and double-blind review, rebuttals, and reviewer discussion workflows for the review phase of technical conferences.

- **Long-standing committee relationships**
Committees at SC, SIGGRAPH, and similar conferences have run their review cycles on it for years.

- **Conference logistics add-ons**
Signage, space planning, and student volunteer tracking, useful if a review vendor also handling signage matters to you.


### The narrow case

- Submission and review is the entire scope, this cycle and every cycle, with no speaker operations worth software
- The committee prefers filing vendor requests for form and report changes over editing them directly
- Signage and student volunteer logistics outweigh speaker experience and content capture in the buying decision

The way forward

## Sessionboard — program, CRM & content

- **Everything Linklings does at review time**
Call for papers (CFP), custom forms, multi-round blind review, rebuttal workflows, conflict-of-interest tracking, and committee assignment run natively. Switching costs zero review rigor.

- **Speaker CRM across years**
Faculty, bios, materials, communications, and participation history persist across editions and events. Returning speakers see prefilled profiles, and the committee sees who delivered.

- **Speaker portal and readiness**
Accepted presenters get tasks, deadlines, AV specs, and uploads in one portal with automated reminders. Linklings has no equivalent; this work lives in email.

- **Content capture built in**
Live transcription, captions, Media Library, AI clips, Attendee Hub, and Create run on the session record. The program becomes a year-round library instead of ending at the closing keynote.

- **Self-serve platform, no request queue**
Committees change forms, workflows, and reports themselves the moment they need to. Vendor-managed systems put a person between you and every tweak.

- **Awards and advocacy on the same graph**
Awards programs, tracked peer shares from speakers and sponsors, and registration attribution reuse the same contact records. No second system, no re-import.


### When Sessionboard wins

- The program needs anything after acceptance: speaker readiness, materials, portal, communications, or content
- Faculty return year over year and the committee wants their history, not an annual blank import
- Review rigor matters and so does everything review rigor feeds: the meeting, the content, the community
- The team wants to configure workflows itself, same day, without a vendor in the loop
- Awards, advocacy, or multi-event reporting are on the roadmap, because a review tool will never grow into them

## Frequently asked questions

Evaluating Sessionboard against Linklings for the program layer — not a forced rip-and-replace.

Is Sessionboard a full Linklings replacement?▼

Yes. Call for papers (CFP), custom submission forms, single- and double-blind review, rebuttals, conflict-of-interest tracking, and committee workflows are native. Committees switch without giving up review rigor, and gain the speaker CRM, portal, and content layer that Linklings does not offer.

Can we keep Linklings for peer review and add Sessionboard?▼

You can run the split mid-contract, and accepted submissions import cleanly. Most teams consolidate at renewal instead: two systems mean two logins, two imports, and a seam in the data exactly where speaker handoff happens. Since Sessionboard runs the same blind review natively, the second system stops earning its keep.

We just signed with Linklings for next year. When does it make sense to look again?▼

Now, in parallel. Speaker CRM, portal, and content capture do not touch the review contract, so this year's faculty data can build value immediately while review finishes where it is. By renewal the committee is already working in one system and the consolidation decision makes itself.

Does Sessionboard handle double-blind review and conflict of interest?▼

Yes, natively: blind review modes, conflict-of-interest tracking, rebuttal workflows, and multi-round decisions. Bring your current review configuration to the demo and we will rebuild it live.

What happens to our speaker history from past years?▼

It becomes the starting point. Past participation, bios, and materials import into the speaker CRM, so returning faculty see prefilled profiles instead of a blank form.

How does pricing compare?▼

Linklings does not publish pricing; quotes follow scope and services. Sessionboard pricing follows meeting and submission volume, and a scoped quote takes one demo. Put it beside your renewal before you sign it.
