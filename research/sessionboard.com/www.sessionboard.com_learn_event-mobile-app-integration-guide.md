## Quick answer

Event mobile apps deliver attendee schedules, maps, and push notifications. Sessionboard holds the authoritative session and speaker graph — abstract acceptance through publish. This guide compares coexistence patterns with Sherpa, Amego, Guidebook, SpotMe, EventPilot, and EventMobi; when to keep the app vendor; and how program changes should flow without spreadsheet round-trips.

## Sound like your team?

Match your role to the job Sessionboard solves — program ops, content reuse, or marketing attribution.

### Program manager

Chairs and reviewers still coordinate in email — you need committee ops on the program graph, not another submission form.

### Speaker operations

Speaker data resets every submission cycle — you need one faculty record from acceptance through show day.

### Marketing director

Social shares without registration attribution — Advocacy tracks peer links; Dispatch covers internal team distribution.

### Content marketing

Transcripts and clips live in a separate tool — you need capture, Attendee Hub, and Create on the session record.

## Vendor landscape

No single winner — match the tool to your trigger, data ownership, and team workflow.

| Vendor | Best when | Tradeoffs | Pricing note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sherpa | White-label enterprise apps for association portfolios with dedicated app teams. | Sherpa owns mobile UX; program data must sync from Sessionboard before each build. | Per-event or portfolio app licensing. |
| Amego | Branded mobile apps with engagement features for mid-market and enterprise events. | App vendor relationship separate from abstract review and speaker CRM depth. | Event app platform pricing. |
| Guidebook | Established Guidebook deployments with schedule and map requirements. | Program ops often still outside Guidebook for complex review workflows. | Guidebook event app tiers. |
| SpotMe | Enterprise life-sciences and corporate events with SpotMe engagement stack. | Registration and app layer may coexist; program graph still needs authoritative source. | Enterprise event app pricing. |
| Whova (all-in-one) | Smaller associations wanting registration, app, and light abstract intake in one contract. | Program depth, review rounds, and speaker CRM often outgrow app-first packaging — see compare page. | Bundled EMS pricing. |
| Sessionboard (program layer) | Multi-round CFP, speaker materials, transcription, Attendee Hub, and Advocacy on one CRM. | Does not replace attendee mobile app — feeds it via export or API. | Program layer scoped to submission and session volume. |

## Evaluation checklist

- Which system owns session times, rooms, and speaker names after acceptance?
- How do last-minute agenda changes reach the app — API, export, or manual re-key?
- Does speaker CRM persist year over year or reset each event build?
- Where do abstract review rounds happen — inside the app vendor or elsewhere?
- Will Attendee Hub, replays, or transcripts live inside the app or link out?
- Is registration attribution required for faculty promotion beyond app engagement metrics?

## Frequently asked questions

Does Sessionboard replace our event app?▼

No — Sessionboard is program system of record; Sherpa, Amego, Guidebook, and SpotMe own attendee mobile UX.

What data should sync to the app?▼

Sessions, speakers, times, rooms, and published agenda state — the fields app builds require.

Can we keep Whova for registration and app?▼

Yes for smaller events; complex programs often add Sessionboard beside Whova — see Sessionboard vs Whova.

How often should we sync?▼

After major publish milestones and whenever chairs change sessions — not once at app freeze only.

What about Attendee Hub and replays?▼

Often deep-linked from the app to Sessionboard-hosted content — design per event requirements.
