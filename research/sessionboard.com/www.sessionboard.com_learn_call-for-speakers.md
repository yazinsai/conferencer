## Quick answer

A call for speakers is an open invitation for people to propose themselves as presenters at an event. Running one takes five stages: publish topics and criteria, collect structured applications, score them against a rubric, notify and confirm those you pick, then collect bios and materials. Unlike a call for abstracts, it recruits the person rather than reviewing written research.

## Running the call?

The same five stages look different depending on which seat you are in — match the guide to the problem in front of you.

### Conference producer

You need a program that sells tickets — the call has to attract recognizable speakers, not just willing ones.

### Education / content committee

Applications arrive uneven and off-topic — the criteria and form decide review quality before anyone starts scoring.

### Speaker operations

Selection is where the work starts — confirmations, bios, headshots, AV needs, and deadlines all land on you.

### Chapter or community lead

You run a call every cycle with a volunteer committee and no budget — it has to be repeatable without a platform contract.

## Evaluation checklist

- Does the announcement say what a strong session looks like, with example topics — or will vague themes attract pitches you cannot use?
- Does the form ask for the few things that predict a good session (a specific takeaway, format, prior speaking evidence) rather than just a title and bio?
- Is the window realistic — four to eight weeks open, with decisions before speakers commit their calendars elsewhere?
- Have you written the scoring criteria before applications open, so decisions survive a rejected applicant asking why?
- Can you spot sales pitches disguised as sessions, and have you said in the announcement that they will be declined?
- Is there a plan for the people you decline — feedback, a smaller slot, or a panel seat — so they apply again next cycle?
- At selection, do confirmations, bios, headshots, and AV requirements flow from the same record, or does a second data chase begin?
- Does the speaker history persist so next year's call starts with people you already know are good?

## Frequently asked questions

What is a call for speakers?▼

An open invitation for people to propose themselves as presenters at an event. Organizers publish the topics, formats, and dates they are looking for, collect applications through a form, score them against criteria, and select a lineup. It is also called a call for proposals or a call for presenters.

What is the difference between a call for speakers and a call for papers?▼

A call for speakers recruits the person — common for keynotes, panels, and industry conference tracks, where prior speaking ability matters. A call for papers or abstracts asks for the content first as a written proposal reviewed on merit, standard for scientific and academic programs. Many conferences run both against one program timeline.

How long should a call for speakers stay open?▼

Four to eight weeks is typical, with the deadline three to five months before the event so selection, confirmations, and travel booking all have room. Expect most applications in the final seventy-two hours no matter how long the window is.

What should a speaker application form ask for?▼

Session title, the specific thing an attendee will be able to do afterward, preferred format and length, topic track, a short bio, and evidence of prior speaking such as a recording or past event. Ask about commercial interest directly, and collect anything the program needs later so you are not chasing it in month three.

How do you choose between speaker applications fairly?▼

Write the criteria before the call opens — relevance to the track, clarity of the takeaway, evidence the person can deliver, and lineup balance — then score every application against them, ideally with more than one reviewer. A written rubric is what lets you answer a declined applicant honestly.

How do speakers find calls for speakers?▼

Through event websites directly, speaker platforms and listing sites, association and chapter newsletters, and community roundups in their field. If you are applying, follow the stated format exactly and lead with the concrete takeaway rather than your biography.

Is there software for running a call for speakers?▼

For small calls, a form and a spreadsheet genuinely work. At volume, or when a committee scores applications, you need assignment and scoring in one place — Sessionize is strong for sourcing and selection in tech and community events, suite modules like Cvent depend on vendor configuration, and Sessionboard runs the call plus everything after selection on one record.

Should you pay speakers who apply through a call?▼

Practice varies by field: association and community events often cover travel only, while commercial conferences pay keynotes and not breakout speakers. Whatever you do, state it in the announcement — unstated compensation is the most common reason a selected speaker withdraws late.
